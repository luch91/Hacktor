Status: preregistration protocol. A free Together Idea Card and one structurally valid free nine-card slate have been observed, along with unsuccessful free requests. No semantically accepted slate, independent reviewer score or paid call has been completed. The paid slate prompt is frozen as `m2a-pilot-v2` and the six-brief fixture SHA-256 is `334808F84262ECAFF3034FFB6A4951419BE20E98D5EBBE333B2FA9A0750FA5A1`. ## Order and input control

1. Freeze the six synthetic, non-holdout briefs in [`pilot-briefs.json`](../../evaluation/model-bakeoff/fixtures/pilot-briefs.json). They cover civic resilience, agriculture, education, health operations, financial access and developer tools, including offline, accessibility, privacy, unavailable data, mandatory technology or rules, short build time and judging criteria. If expanded to eight briefs, commit and hash them before output inspection.
2. Check current catalog IDs, exact prices, upstream capabilities, account quotas and free designation. Use `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and `TOGETHER_API_KEY` only from ignored local environment files. No paid calls until a separate numeric owner approval.
3. Run one minimal Idea Card compatibility request on at most two current free OpenRouter models and Together's free Ternary Bonsai, using the same synthetic fixture. A requested free slug absent from the live catalog is skipped, not replaced silently. Keep free usage within published quotas and record every attempt, including failures.
4. If free models pass schema and basic constraint screening, run at most one nine-card pilot slate per free model on two briefs before spending more free quota. Only after human review identify paid finalists. Do not run nine full eight-stage sessions for every catalog candidate.
5. After a separately approved paid cap, select no more than four paid model/provider combinations for six briefs, balancing inexpensive candidates and a stronger reasoning candidate. Use the same task, prompt, schema, reasoning settings where supported, input information and output cap. Pin OpenRouter upstream or record and audit any owner-approved routing variation. No automatic cross-model fallback.
6. Blind at least two reviewers to model/provider labels where practical. Preserve every synthetic response, error, usage record and adjudication in ignored local output, then publish only sanitized summaries and reproducible hashes. Do not tune these prompts on any of the formal 48 briefs.

## Slate quality rubric

Score each dimension 0 to 4. A 0 means absent, violated or unusable; 2 means a plausible but ordinary or thinly explained attempt; 4 means concrete, challenge-specific, buildable and causally differentiated. Reviewers must describe the problem, beneficiary, mechanism and demo before awarding 3 or 4. Dimensions: problem originality, mechanism originality and diversity, challenge relevance, technical feasibility, hard-constraint compliance, demonstrability within hackathon time, assumptions and risk analysis, and usefulness to a builder. A title or strategy label change never counts as meaningful differentiation. Flag generic chatbot or dashboard substitutions, invented data or dependencies, superficial rewording and unsupported originality claims. A critical mandatory-rule violation disqualifies a slate regardless of average score.

An **accepted slate** has nine valid strategy cards, no critical constraint violation, at least seven distinct mechanisms after human normalization, at least three beneficiary groups or substantively different beneficiary roles, no invented required dataset or dependency, and mean reviewer scores of at least 3 for feasibility, challenge relevance and overall usefulness. Both reviewers must agree that at least three cards have substantive differences in problem, beneficiary or causal mechanism. Record disagreements and the adjudicator separately. These are pilot screening rules, not changes to the formal M2A gate.

## Metrics and ranking

For each exact model and upstream, record attempted requests, accepted slates, first-attempt success, schema validity, critical violations, duplicate cards, mean originality, mechanism diversity and feasibility, retry count, median and p95 latency, input/output/reasoning tokens, and actual provider debit. Cost per accepted slate is **all actual inference charges for that candidate, including failed calls and retries, divided by accepted slates**. If none are accepted, report undefined with the total spend. A zero-token-cost model still has latency, quota, review and operational costs.

For eligible models, propose a 100-point weighted score: 30 originality and substantive differentiation, 25 technical feasibility and challenge relevance, 20 hard-constraint compliance, 15 reliability and structured output, and 10 cost per accepted slate and latency. Normalize each dimension on the predeclared 0 to 4 scale, then report raw scores alongside the weighted score. Any critical hard-constraint violation, unsupported required data or unstable schema may disqualify the candidate. With six briefs, scores are directional and uncertainty is high; do not claim a statistically established winner or a production reliability rate.

Do not recommend mixed stage routing from catalog prices alone. Compare a single-model slate with a proposed split on the same briefs only if pilot budget remains and owner approves the extra calls. The formal evaluation keeps one model and settings matched across Hacktor and generic arms regardless of pilot routing hypotheses.
